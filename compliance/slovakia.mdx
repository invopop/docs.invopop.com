## Executive summary

From **1 January 2027**, VAT payers established in Slovakia must issue structured e-invoices for their domestic business (B2B) and public sector (B2G) sales. Every business and legal entity that can receive such an invoice must be able to receive it. The mandate, called **eFaktúra**, comes from an amendment to the Slovak VAT Act (Act 385/2025, which introduces §85o into Act 222/2004).

Slovakia builds on the [Peppol](/apps/peppol) network and adds one obligation:

1. **Exchange** the invoice over Peppol, in the standard **Peppol BIS Billing 3.0** format. There is no Slovak profile on top of it.
2. **Report** the invoice data to the Financial Administration (Finančná správa). The certified providers on each side send a separate tax data document to a tax authority endpoint for every invoice.

Invoices are sent and received through certified providers, locally called **digital postmen**. This adds the tax authority as a fifth corner to the usual Peppol four-corner network, similar to [France](/compliance/france).

[Domestic e-invoicing](#domestic-e-invoicing-b2b-b2g) is voluntary during 2026 and mandatory from January 2027. [Cross-border](#e-reporting) transactions follow from **1 July 2030**, the same day the periodic VAT control statement is abolished. Consumer (B2C) sales are not in scope.

The standard VAT rate is **23%**, with reduced rates of **19%** and **5%**.

## Invoicing in Slovakia

Every invoice travels twice: once to the buyer over Peppol, and once, as tax data, to the Financial Administration. Both providers take part. The seller's provider reports the invoice as issued, and the buyer's provider reports it as received.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Invoice created">
    The supplier's system sends the invoice to its digital postman, which builds a Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 invoice.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send over Peppol">
    The supplier's postman looks up the buyer on Peppol by its Slovak tax number (DIČ) and sends the invoice to the buyer's postman.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deliver to the buyer">
    The buyer's postman delivers the invoice to the buyer's accounting system or app.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Report as issued">
    At the same time, the supplier's postman sends the invoice data to the Financial Administration as a tax data document.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Report as received">
    The buyer's postman reports the same invoice as received, within 5 days of receiving it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Domestic e-invoicing (B2B, B2G)">
    VAT payers established in Slovakia must issue an e-invoice for domestic supplies of goods and services to other businesses and to public bodies. The buyer's consent is not required. All legal entities and taxable persons, including sole traders, the self-employed and people who rent out property, must be able to receive e-invoices, which in practice means contracting a digital postman.

    |                     |                                                                                                   |
    | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | **Scope**           | B2B, B2G (domestic)                                                                               |
    | **Format**          | Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 (UBL 2.1), no Slovak profile                                               |
    | **Compliance**      | e-invoicing and e-reporting                                                                       |
    | **Infrastructure**  | Peppol network through certified providers (digital postmen)                                      |
    | **Model**           | Decentralized, with the tax authority as a fifth corner                                           |
    | **Effective date**  | Voluntary during **2026**; mandatory from **1 January 2027**                                      |
    | **Agency**          | Finančná správa SR (Financial Administration), also the Slovak Peppol Authority                   |
    | **Invopop support** | Work in progress                                                                                  |
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Reporting to the Financial Administration">
    The reporting is not a separate filing by the business. The digital postmen on both sides send a **tax data document** to the Financial Administration for each invoice, alongside the Peppol exchange. The supplier's side reports at issuance, and the buyer's side within 5 days of receipt. This near-real-time data replaces the periodic VAT control statement from July 2030.

    |                     |                                                                        |
    | ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
    | **Scope**           | B2B, B2G (reported by both the supplier's and the buyer's provider)    |
    | **Format**          | Peppol tax data document (TDD-SK)                                      |
    | **Channel**         | Sent by the certified provider to a tax authority endpoint on Peppol   |
    | **Deadline**        | Supplier at issuance; buyer within 5 days of receipt                   |
    | **Effective date**  | Mandatory from **1 January 2027**                                      |
    | **Agency**          | Finančná správa SR (Financial Administration)                          |
    | **Invopop support** | Work in progress                                                      |
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is out of scope">
    No e-invoice is required for:

    * **Simplified invoices**: receipts up to EUR 100, and eKasa (cash register) receipts up to EUR 400 including VAT.
    * **VAT-exempt supplies** (under §28 to §43 and §47 of the VAT Act), such as exempt rental of property.
    * **Supplies between members of the same VAT group**, which are not subject to VAT.
    * **Consumer (B2C) sales.**

    Classified supplies, for example to the Slovak intelligence services, must not be issued as e-invoices at all.

    Companies registered for VAT in Slovakia but not established there are excluded from the obligation to issue and receive e-invoices until **30 June 2030**.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Invoicing outside Peppol">
    Peppol through a digital postman is the default channel. An e-invoice may be sent another way, for example over an existing EDI connection, if the recipient agrees. The agreement can take any form, including simply accepting and paying the invoice. The invoice must still be a structured e-invoice in UBL 2.1 or UN/CEFACT CII that complies with EN 16931. A recipient who receives an invoice over Peppol must accept it there.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## E-reporting

Until 30 June 2030, VAT payers keep filing the periodic **VAT control statement** (kontrolný výkaz) and the summary statement for intra-EU supplies. From **1 July 2030**, both are abolished. Invoice data then reaches the Financial Administration automatically through the digital postmen, and the mandate extends to cross-border transactions, in line with the EU's VAT in the Digital Age (ViDA) initiative.

## Regulation

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Invoice format and content">
    A Slovak e-invoice is a structured XML file that complies with the European **EN 16931** standard, in UBL 2.1 or UN/CEFACT CII syntax. Over Peppol, the format is **Peppol BIS Billing 3.0** (UBL). Slovakia adds no national business rules to it.

    * A PDF or a scanned image is **not** an e-invoice. It may travel as an attachment to the XML, as can a payment QR code.
    * The deadline to issue an invoice stays at **15 days** from the supply of goods or services. It also applies to corrective invoices.
    * **Self-billing** is allowed under a written agreement between supplier and buyer. The buyer issues the invoice and sends it through its own postman, using the dedicated self-billing document type.
    * **Corrections**: the Financial Administration recommends a credit note followed by a new invoice. A corrective invoice must reference the original (BT-25). Support for the corrective invoice type code 384 is being prepared with OpenPeppol for changes that don't affect VAT or price.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Identifiers">
    Slovak businesses are reached on Peppol by their **DIČ** (tax identification number, 10 digits), under the participant identifier scheme **0245**. For example: `0245:1234567890`. The VAT number scheme 9950 is not used for the mandate, so a business already listed under 9950 needs a 0245 registration with a Slovak provider.

    The **IČ DPH** (VAT number: `SK` followed by 10 digits) goes in the invoice itself as the seller's and buyer's VAT identifier. Every VAT payer has a DIČ, but not every DIČ holder has a VAT number.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="VAT rates">
    Slovakia applies the EU VAT framework. The current rates apply since 1 January 2025.

    | Rate | Percentage | Application |
    |------|-----------|-------------|
    | Standard | **23%** | Most goods and services |
    | Reduced | **19%** | Non-basic foodstuffs, electricity and certain catering services |
    | Super-reduced | **5%** | Basic foodstuffs, medicines, medical devices, books and accommodation |
    | Zero-rated | **0%** | Exports and intra-EU supplies to VAT-registered buyers |
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Certified providers (digital postmen)">
    Only providers accredited by the Financial Administration, as the Slovak Peppol Authority, can act as digital postmen. A provider must be an OpenPeppol-certified service provider based in an EU member state, with a clean criminal record. It does not need to be Slovak. The Financial Administration publishes the list of certified providers.

    Each business chooses its digital postman on the Financial Administration's portal, which registers the business with that provider. A provider can be reached through accounting or ERP software, or through the provider's own web or mobile app.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Archiving">
    E-invoices must be kept in their **XML** form for **10 years** from the end of the calendar year they relate to. The obligation stays with the business: digital postmen have no legal duty to keep the invoices they transmit.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Penalties">
    Failing to report invoice data, or reporting it incorrectly or late, can be fined up to **EUR 10,000**, and up to **EUR 100,000** for repeated violations. There is no fine for an obvious mistake that is corrected promptly, or for a proven failure of the business's digital postman when the data is reported as soon as the failure is fixed.
  </Accordion>
