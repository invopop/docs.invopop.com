  <Accordion title="Is e-invoicing mandatory in Malaysia?">
    Yes, for most taxpayers. The mandate came in four phases from 1 August 2024 to 1 January 2026, based on turnover in financial year 2022. New businesses come in on 1 July 2026. Taxpayers with annual turnover below RM3 million are exempt.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Which transactions does MyInvois cover?">
    B2B, B2G and B2C transactions. Invoices to public bodies follow the same flow as invoices to businesses. For consumer sales, the supplier can issue a monthly consolidated e-invoice, except for the transactions that must have an individual e-invoice.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is Peppol mandatory in Malaysia?">
    No. Every in-scope e-invoice must go to MyInvois for validation. Peppol, with the PINT-MY profile, is one optional channel to share the validated e-invoice with the buyer. Only MDEC-accredited providers can run a Peppol access point in Malaysia.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Who is exempt?">
    Taxpayers with annual turnover below RM3 million, unless a group company is at or above that amount. Foreign diplomatic offices, individuals who do not carry on a business and statutory authorities for statutory collections are also exempt.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the relaxation period?">
    IRBM gave each phase a six month relaxation period. For Phase 4 businesses, it lasts until 31 December 2027. During this period, a taxpayer can put all its transactions in consolidated e-invoices and refuse requests for individual e-invoices.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="When can I not use a consolidated e-invoice?">
    From 1 January 2026, you cannot consolidate a single transaction above RM10,000, in any industry. Some activities also need an individual e-invoice, for example sales of motor vehicles, flight tickets, construction contracts, electricity and telecommunication services.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What are the penalties?">
    If you do not issue an e-invoice, the fine is RM200 to RM20,000, or up to 6 months in prison, or both. The penalty applies to each non-compliance.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does Malaysia have VAT?">
    No. Malaysia repealed GST in 2018. The transaction taxes are Sales Tax and Service Tax (SST). The e-invoice shows the tax type with a code from the official list.
  </Accordion>
