  <Accordion title="What format does a Malaysian e-invoice use?">
    UBL 2.1, in XML or JSON, submitted to the MyInvois API. On the Peppol network, the format is PINT-MY. The guideline lists 55 data fields for an e-invoice.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What does MyInvois return after validation?">
    A Unique Identifier Number, a validation link and a QR code. Validation usually takes less than 2 seconds.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I still send a PDF to my buyer?">
    Yes. You can share the validated e-invoice or a readable copy of it, such as a PDF. The readable copy must show the QR code that links to the validation page on MyInvois.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I correct an e-invoice?">
    You can cancel a validated e-invoice within 72 hours. After that, issue a credit note, a debit note or a refund note. A refund note is for money that goes back to the buyer.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can the buyer reject an e-invoice?">
    The buyer can ask for a rejection within 72 hours of validation, with a reason. The request notifies the supplier. The supplier then decides whether to cancel the e-invoice in the same 72 hours.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Which TIN do I use for a foreign buyer or a consumer?">
    Use a general TIN. Use `EI00000000010` for the general public and `EI00000000020` for a foreign buyer. Use `EI00000000030` for a foreign supplier in a self-billed e-invoice, and `EI00000000040` for a public body that has no TIN.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="When must I issue a self-billed e-invoice?">
    In defined cases, the buyer issues the e-invoice. Examples are purchases from foreign suppliers, payments to agents and dealers, e-commerce transactions and payments to individuals who do not carry on a business. Self-billed documents use type codes 11 to 14.
  </Accordion>
