  <Accordion title="Is e-invoicing mandatory in Croatia?">
    Yes. Under Fiscalization 2.0 (eRačun), VAT-registered businesses established in Croatia must issue and receive structured e-invoices since January 2026. They must also report each invoice to the tax authority. Businesses that are not VAT-registered must receive e-invoices since January 2026 and issue them from January 2027.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What are the two obligations under Fiscalization 2.0?">
    First, exchange a structured e-invoice with the recipient over Croatia's 4-corner network. Second, report each invoice to the tax authority's Fiscalization System (CIS), separately from the exchange. The payment or the rejection of the invoice is reported later.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does Fiscalization 2.0 apply to foreign companies?">
    No. The obligation covers only domestic transactions between businesses with their seat, residence or habitual residence in Croatia. A foreign supplier issues invoices to Croatian businesses on paper or in another form that the VAT rules accept. To invoice a Croatian public body, a foreign supplier uses Peppol.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I send B2G invoices to Croatian public bodies over Peppol?">
    Only if your business is established outside Croatia. A foreign supplier sends a Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 invoice to FINA's Peppol access point, with the recipient ID `9934:` plus the public body's OIB. A Croatian supplier sends the invoice over the Croatian network in the HR CIUS profile, the same way as to a business. It also reports the invoice to the tax authority.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I use a plain Peppol invoice in Croatia?">
    Not for a domestic Croatian transaction. Croatia uses the European EN 16931 standard with its own national profile (HR CIUS), and a Peppol BIS Billing 3.0 invoice does not meet it. Croatia's network uses the same technology as Peppol, but it is a separate network with its own directory. The only Peppol route is from a foreign supplier to a public body.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="When must a received invoice be reported?">
    The recipient reports a received invoice to the tax authority within 5 working days. The supplier reports it at issue. Payments of outgoing invoices and rejections of incoming invoices are reported by the 20th of the following month.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How are consumer (B2C) sales handled?">
    Consumer sales stay on the older receipt system, a separate connection to the same tax authority backend. The point of sale signs each receipt, reports it in real time, and prints it with an identifier, a security code and a QR code. Since January 2026, this applies to all payment methods, not only cash.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How long must invoices be kept in Croatia?">
    Keep e-invoices for 6 years in their original structured form.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What are the penalties for non-compliance?">
    Fines go up to EUR 66,360 for companies. Software providers are responsible for the compliance of their software. No grace period applies.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What VAT rates apply in Croatia?">
    A standard rate of 25%, with reduced rates of 13% and 5%. Croatia uses the euro.
  </Accordion>
