  <Accordion title="What format does a Croatian e-invoice use?">
    The European EN 16931 standard with the Croatian national profile (HR CIUS), in UBL 2.1 or CII format. Each invoice line must also have a product classification code (KPD).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What is the KPD code?">
    KPD is Croatia's product classification. Each invoice line must have a KPD code. Credit notes and prepayment invoices do not need it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How do I find where a Croatian business receives invoices?">
    Each recipient has one receiving address in the national directory (AMS). The supplier's intermediary looks up the recipient in AMS and sends the invoice to the access point that AMS returns. For a public body, that access point is FINA's central platform.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I invoice in a currency other than euros?">
    Yes, but all amounts reported to the tax authority must be in euros (EUR).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How does a customer reject an invoice?">
    The customer sends an application response message to the supplier. It then reports the rejection to the tax authority by the 20th of the following month. A customer that rejects an invoice cannot deduct its VAT.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need to be based in Croatia to provide e-invoicing services there?">
    No. A company that sends or reports invoices for other businesses must be certified by the tax authority as an information intermediary. It does not have to be established in Croatia, and several foreign providers are on the official list.
  </Accordion>
